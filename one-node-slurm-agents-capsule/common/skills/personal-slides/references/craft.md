# General craft (lowest priority)

Apply these only where `feedback.md` and `style-profile.md` say nothing.

## Argument

- One message per slide. The title states it; the body supports it. A reader skimming only the titles should follow the talk.
- Order by what the audience needs next: problem before method, setup before results, the strongest evidence first.
- Every figure or table on a slide is there to prove the title; say in one line what to look at.
- Results carry their conditions: dataset, metric, number of runs, variation, baseline. Never present a single cherry-picked number as the result.
- Cite on the slide where a claim or figure comes from someone else.

## Density

- If the text needs a smaller font than the user's usual body size, split the slide or cut text; do not shrink.
- Prefer a figure with a takeaway over a paragraph describing the figure.
- Backup slides hold detail that the main line does not need.

## Visual consistency

- Use the template's placeholders and positions so titles do not jump between slides.
- Keep emphasis rare enough to mean something; one accent color for "ours/important".
- Align edges with neighbouring shapes; keep the margins of the model slide.
- Figures keep their aspect ratio and remain legible at presentation distance (axis labels near body size).

## PowerPoint file hygiene

- Bullets come from paragraph levels, not typed "・" or "•" characters.
- Line breaks within a bullet are rare; a new idea is a new paragraph.
- Speaker notes go in notes, never in a hidden text box.
- Replace the content of a copied slide completely; leftover text, figures or notes from the model are defects.
